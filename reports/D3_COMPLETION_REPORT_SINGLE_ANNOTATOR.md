## 3. Architecture and pipeline

```
data/processed/reviews.csv (1,500 reviews, 15 products) -> 299-review annotation sample
  [1] EXTRACT    qwen2.5:3b few-shot -> ReviewRecord (pipeline/extraction.py)            reused from D2, unchanged
  [2] AGGREGATE  pandas: sentiment, ratings, aspect +/-, complaints, evidence quotes      pipeline/aggregation.py
  [3] SUMMARISE  qwen2.5:3b, statistics + evidence quotes -> ProductSummaryContent        pipeline/summarization.py
  [4] DECOMPOSE  summary -> verbatim claims with their cited ids                         evaluation/claims.py
  [5] CHECK      numbers, counts, denominators, majorities, aspects, citations (no LLM)  evaluation/claim_checks.py
  [6] VERIFY     llama3.2: ONE claim vs the full text of ITS cited reviews, verbatim quote evaluation/judge_v4.py
  [7] COMBINE    worse verdict wins; unverified quote -> UNVERIFIABLE; scores derived      schemas/claim_schema.py
  [8] RUBRIC     faithfulness / coverage / consistency / hallucination 1-5, derived        evaluation/rubric_v4.py
  [9] CONTROLS   nc-v3: 123 planted + 123 matched clean claims, frozen before judging     evaluation/negative_control_v3.py

 [11] STATUS     completion criteria recomputed from files -> dashboard badge             evaluation/d3_status.py
```

## 4. Extraction and aggregation methodology

Each review of the D2 annotation sample is extracted by qwen2.5:3b with the few-shot prompt selected in D2 into a Pydantic `ReviewRecord` (overall sentiment, aspects with polarity and a verbatim evidence span, complaint categories), with deterministic fixes and at most two repair calls. The extraction file is reused unchanged from D2 (285 valid, 14 stored failures). `pipeline/aggregation.py` then computes, per product and without any LLM: sentiment counts and percentages, average rating, aspect mentions split into positive / negative / neutral, complaint counts and percentages (denominator = all reviews of the product), the number of reviews with complaints, a yearly trend and up to ten evidence quotes. Judge v4 re-reads these statistics for every numeric check, so statistics are faithful to the extractions by construction; D2 measured complaint micro-precision 0.367, so complaint statistics can be inflated relative to the review text.

## 5. Summary generation methodology

qwen2.5:3b (Ollama, local) receives the statistics JSON and the evidence quotes (not the full reviews) and returns `ProductSummaryContent`: an overall opinion, up to six appreciated points and six complaints (each citing review ids that must belong to the evidence set, enforced by the schema), frequent aspects, improvement areas and recommendations. The 15 summaries in `outputs/live/summaries/summaries.jsonl` are the evaluated artefacts and were never modified; corrections are separate copies (section 8).

## 6. Judge v4 methodology and claim-level results

Judge model `ollama/llama3.2` (a different family from the qwen2.5 summariser), temperature 0.0, num_ctx 4096, num_predict 384, constrained decoding True, 1 call per claim, 0 repairs, prompt SHA-256 `988ef24e271e7983fe402b4e878de35f035b3d4f46470db5490fb760fafe7471`. Every overall-opinion sentence, appreciated/complaint point, frequent-aspect list, improvement area and recommendation becomes one verbatim claim. Statistic sentences are checked deterministically; each cited point is checked by the LLM against the full text of its cited reviews only and must return a verbatim quote, which must be found in those reviews or the verdict becomes UNVERIFIABLE. The deterministic and LLM verdicts are combined (the worse wins; a deterministic failure cannot be overridden) and the score is computed from the statuses:

`faithfulness = 100 * max(0, (S + 0.5*P + 0*U - 1*C) / (S + P + U + C)); UNVERIFIABLE claims are excluded from the denominator and reported as (1 - evidence_coverage). Caps: any CONTRADICTED claim -> score <= 60; any UNSUPPORTED claim -> score <= 80; 'perfect' (100) only if every verifiable claim is SUPPORTED.`

Results on the 15 originals: 239 claims — SUPPORTED 142, PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED 29, UNSUPPORTED 44, CONTRADICTED 9, UNVERIFIABLE 15; by method deterministic 155, llm 84; numeric claims 18 (5 failed a deterministic check); validity CONTRADICTED_CLAIMS 5, UNSUPPORTED_CLAIMS 10; mean faithfulness 64.1 / 100. LLM calls: 258 valid, 0 validation failures, mean latency 17.9 s.

The per-claim table (status, method, quote, numeric calculation, explanation) is `outputs/live/d3/judge_v4/dataset__few_shot__qwen2.5-3b__v1/claims.csv`; the 16 investigation-known errors and the 53 flagged claims are listed with explanations in `reports/final/D3_Final_Report.md` §12 and §17.

## 7. Anchored 1-5 rubric (derived, no LLM scores)

Rubric version `v4-derived-2026-11-16.r2`; 0 LLM calls; invariant violations: 0. Anchors (written before computing, following `prompts/judge.py::RUBRIC`; revised once after the first computation, see section 12):

- **faithfulness**: band of the derived 0-100 faithfulness score: 5 >= 95, 4 >= 80, 3 >= 60, 2 >= 40, else 1 (schemas/claim_schema.py::derive_scores)
- **coverage**: themes = top-3 aspects (excluding 'other') by mentions and top-3 complaint categories, each mentioned by >= 2 reviews; the rank-1 aspect and rank-1 complaint are MAJOR, the others MINOR. 5 = every theme mentioned; 4 = misses exactly one minor theme; 3 = misses a major theme or >= 2 minor themes, but mentions >= 50% of themes; 2 = mentions >= 25% of themes; 1 = mentions fewer than 25%
- **consistency**: per statistic-stating claim, the worst deterministic statistic finding: 'fail' = MAJOR mismatch, 'misleading' = MINOR mismatch; a failed numeric check = WRONG NUMBER. 5 = no mismatch; 4 = exactly one minor mismatch; 3 = one major mismatch without a wrong number, or >= 2 minor mismatches; 2 = a wrong number or 2 major mismatches; 1 = >= 3 major mismatches
- **hallucination**: n = UNSUPPORTED + CONTRADICTED claims (all claims). 5 = n == 0; 4 = n == 1 and it is UNSUPPORTED; 3 = n <= 3 with at most one CONTRADICTED and no failed number; 2 = n <= 6 and no failed number; 1 = n > 6 or any number with a wrong value (numeric check severity 'error': a fabricated or misattributed statistic); misleading denominators are not wrong values

## 12. Limitations

- Human validation uses ONE annotator: the comparison measures agreement with that person, not inter-annotator reliability or ground truth; individual misreadings or a personal threshold for 'supported' cannot be detected without a second annotator. The annotator is a project member who may have seen earlier judge outputs and investigation reports, so labelling is blind only within the interface.
- Small local judge (llama3.2 3B): on clean controls the LLM layer flags 12 of 63 correct claims; some of the 44 UNSUPPORTED findings in the originals are therefore false positives. 3 of 8 misleading paraphrases passed.
- Negative controls are template-based ('A reviewer reports that ...') and per-type samples are small (5-15).
- Statistic types are detected by checks written by the same author as the generator.
- Statistics inherit extraction errors (D2 complaint precision 0.367); summaries can be faithful to statistics that are not faithful to the reviews (B001DHKBXU).
- Opinion sentences are checked against statistics only; derived claims only for negative evidence on the targeted aspect.
- Rubric anchors (coverage, consistency, hallucination) were set by the AI assistant and are not calibrated against human ratings.
