### D2 — what the comparison shows

- **Few-shot is clearly best** on the primary metric: overall-sentiment macro-F1 **0.6025**, against 0.4380 for zero-shot and 0.4232 for Chain-of-Thought (accuracy 0.8127 vs 0.6120 and 0.5753). It is also best on aspect detection (micro-F1 0.6488 vs 0.5221 / 0.5028), aspect sentiment (macro-F1 0.6479), joint aspect-sentiment pairs (0.5579) and complaint micro-F1 (0.4153), and it has the highest output validity (95.3%) and first-attempt validity (94.3%).
- **The main failure mode of zero-shot and CoT is over-predicting `neutral`.** Zero-shot predicted neutral for 79 reviews and CoT for 94, although only 5 reference labels are neutral; 44 (zero-shot) and 51 (CoT) reference-positive reviews were labelled neutral. Few-shot predicted neutral only 7 times. The demonstrations calibrate what "neutral" means far better than the written label definitions alone.
- **`mixed` is the hardest class for every strategy** (recall 0.095 / 0.310 / 0.071). Mixed reviews are most often predicted as negative (few-shot: 19 of 42), i.e. the model lets the complaint dominate.
- **The CoT procedure did not help this 3B model.** It raised macro precision (0.7201) only because it rarely predicts mixed, while recall on positive fell to 0.4595. Its silent reasoning instructions add prompt length without improving calibration; it also produced the most output failures (20).
- **Cost:** few-shot is the most expensive per review (mean 2,884.47 input tokens, 53.8 s) because of the five demonstrations; zero-shot is the cheapest (2,057 tokens, 41.2 s). On this hardware the accuracy gain is worth the ~30% extra time.
- **Failures are counted, not hidden:** 17, 14 and 20 reviews had no valid output after two repair attempts; they are scored as wrong. Final failure causes (zero-shot / few-shot / CoT): evidence not verbatim 6 / 5 / 8, out-of-taxonomy label 5 / 5 / 6, evidence longer than 300 characters 4 / 1 / 1, invalid or truncated JSON (including runaway generation at the 1,024-token cap) 0 / 3 / 2, missing or extra fields 2 / 0 / 0, over-long rationale 0 / 0 / 3.

### Reading the numbers correctly

- 298 of the 299 reference labels are AI-assisted; the scores measure agreement with those labels.
- `neutral` has only 5 reference examples, so its per-class F1 is very noisy and pulls macro-F1 down for all strategies.
- The sample is rating-stratified (about 60 reviews per star rating), so accuracy describes this balanced set, not the natural 72%-five-star distribution of the dataset.

### D3 — what the chain and the judge show

- **The summariser was reliable; the judge was not.** qwen2.5:3b produced a valid, citation-grounded summary for all 15 products (14 on the first attempt, 1 after one repair). The local judge (llama3.2) returned a schema-valid verdict for only **5 of 15** summaries; the other 10 were still invalid after two repair attempts — it either gave a bare score per dimension instead of a score plus justification, or nested the `unsupported_claims` list inside every dimension. Those 10 summaries have no judge score; none was filled in, and the failures were not re-sampled.
- **The judge's scores are internally inconsistent.** In the 5 valid verdicts faithfulness is 3–4 while hallucination is 1–2 (1 = "many unsupported claims"), so the two dimensions contradict each other.
- **Negative control: the judge did not detect unfaithful content.** Of the 5 eligible pairs, 3 produced valid verdicts for the corrupted copy (2 corrupted judgements failed validation). The corrupted copy's mean score was lower in all 3 pairs, but only through coverage and consistency (it noticed the removed complaints); **faithfulness was lower in 0 of 3** and the invented "Wi-Fi app / voice assistant" feature was flagged in 0 of 3. The fabricated percentage was flagged in 3 of 3, which the deterministic percentage check also catches.
- **How to read this:** with 3 scored pairs the control is far too small for any statistical claim; it is evidence *against* using this 3B judge's faithfulness and hallucination scores as a quality measure. The deterministic checks (cited ids belong to the product, percentages appear in the statistics, citation polarity, top-aspect coverage) are the more trustworthy D3 evidence in this project. A larger judge or human rating of the summaries would be needed to evaluate faithfulness properly.
