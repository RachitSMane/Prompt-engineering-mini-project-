## 4. Why the strategies differ

- **Zero-shot and CoT over-use "neutral".** Zero-shot labels 44 of the 105 judged positive reviews neutral, and CoT
  51 of 103. A 4- or 5-star review with a small caveat ("works, but…") is read as balanced. CoT's step-by-step
  reasoning makes this worse: listing pros and cons makes the model hedge. Its neutral recall is 1.0, but its
  neutral precision is 0.05.
- **Few-shot fixes the label boundary.** The worked examples show that a positive review with a minor complaint is
  still *positive*. Positive recall rises from about 0.5 to 0.92, and that drives the accuracy gain (0.81). The
  examples also show the expected JSON shape, so first-attempt validity is highest (94.3 %) and aspect detection is
  best (0.65). The cost is about 40 % more input tokens and about 30 % more latency.
- **All strategies miss "mixed".** The best mixed recall is 0.31: mixed reviews are mostly predicted negative.
- **Neutral has only 5 reference examples.** Its F1 is near 0 for every strategy and pulls macro-F1 down. Macro-F1
  is the headline metric because the stratified sample is not balanced by sentiment.
- **Caveat:** the reference labels are AI-assisted. A strategy that "agrees" with them may share the same model
  biases. The ranking needs confirming on human labels.

## 5. Completing D2 (what the reviewer does)

1. Start the app in normal mode, open **Human Annotation → D2 reference dataset**, type your name, tick the
   confirmation and click **Approve Existing Reference Annotations**. This approves the 299 valid, complete records,
   once; a second click changes nothing.
2. Decide the flagged record `R5cfb12ef75b4` ("text is garbled and partly unreadable") under *Individual decision*:
   approve it, correct its sentiment, or exclude it with a reason. The dashboard reports **Ready** only when all 300
   are accepted. Excluding it leaves 299, which is not ready.
3. **Prompt Experiments → Sensitivity analysis — approved-reference evaluation → Evaluate against approved references** (no LLM call). The status
   shows *Complete* when every accepted reference has a prediction from all three strategies and the stdlib
   cross-check passes.

**What approval does not mean:** approval records that a named person accepted a label. Every accepted label keeps
its original provenance (`human_created` or `ai_assisted`), in the store, the export and the evaluation result. The
independent human-label counts (1 created, 0 verified) are shown separately on the same page. **The D2 results are
agreement with approved, mostly AI-assisted reference labels, not with independent human ground truth.**

Commands (no LLM): `scripts
eference_annotations.py validate | status | approve --reviewer "Name" --confirm |
decide <id> approved --reviewer "Name" | export | evaluate --run 20261111-030912_qwen2.5-3b`.

The independent blind human annotation is complete (300 / 300, 2026-11-15); second-person verification is not part of
